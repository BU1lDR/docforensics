# Limitations, assumptions and likely false positives

What this tool cannot do, what it assumes, and which ordinary things make it raise a finding. Every finding is for a human reviewer: the tool reports what it measured and never reaches a conclusion about authenticity or intent. The wording of each finding in the dossier states its own measure and tolerance. This page is the general picture.

## Likely false positives

A finding says that a measurement differs from what a check expects. The table gives, for each detector, what it measures and what else produces the same measurement besides a change to the document. It follows the fixed wording the dossier prints beside each finding (`src/docforensics/explain/templates.py`).

| Detector | What it measures | Ordinary causes of the same result |
|---|---|---|
| Saved revisions (`pdf_revisions`) | Text differs between an earlier saved version kept inside the PDF and a later one | A correction, a re-issue, a form being filled in: any later save |
| Covered or hidden text (`pdf_overlay`) | Text still stored in the file lies under a filled shape or a picture drawn after it, or is invisible, tiny or off the page | Panels, redactions, logos, stamps, forms being filled in |
| Fonts of figures and dates (`pdf_fonts`) | A figure or date set in a different font, size or colour from the others in its column or on its line | A document put together by more than one program, a total or a note set differently on purpose |
| File metadata (`pdf_meta`) | The producer and creator the file names, and how long after its creation it was last modified | Saving a file again. Anyone who can write the file can write this record |
| Raw file structure (`file_structure`) | Bytes after the end of a file or of a picture inside it, an /Info record that differs from the XMP packet, a signature byte range that stops short of the end of the file, embedded files or action keys, an index that had to be rebuilt, an image-editor name or a later save time in picture metadata | A program that pads a file or appends a trailer, saving again in place, a second signature, a countersignature or a form filled in after a signature, a document with an attachment or an /OpenAction (ordinary generators write both), a producer that writes a damaged index, an editor used to crop or resize a genuine photograph or scan. Anyone who can write the file can write every one of these fields |
| Grain of a scan (`scan_noise`) | Words whose image grain differs from the page median | Uneven lighting, a second JPEG save, a stamp, a fold |
| Placement on a scan (`scan_layout`) | A word out of line with its row, a figure whose right edge or height differs from the page's other figures, after the page is straightened | A crease, a curled page, a misread box |
| Compression history of a scan (`ela`) | Part of a JPEG scan has no trace of an earlier JPEG save that the rest of the page carries | A page put together from more than one scan, a stamp, a date or a page number added by the scanner or by a program, a sticker. The check is silent, and says nothing, when the page was saved once, when its saves are close together, when the changed part has the page's own JPEG grid and quality (a part taken from the same place of another scan of the same level is of that kind), or when the earlier save was written with other quantisation tables than libjpeg's. A page whose last save is under quality 40 is not examined, so no region on it is not a result |
| Learned word check on a scan (`learned_patches`) | Words whose weighted score over fourteen measures against the rest of their page (grain, edges, strokes, ink, JPEG block grid, baseline, placement, and distance off the line of the words beside them) reaches a limit fitted to synthetic edits | Uneven lighting or a fold, a stamp or a signature over text, a word set in another typeface, size or weight (a heading, an amount in bold), a field filled in by another program or machine, a scan saved a second time, a curled or skewed page. The weights were fitted to one generator's edits, so a real scanner may move the measures in ways that data did not show |
| Pages without scanner grain (`scan_grain`) | A page read by OCR whose flat paper carries grain on less than 0.5 of its pixels (simulated scans 0.885 to 0.914); the grain, placement and learned-word checks are not applied to it and the document gets one limitation naming the page | A real scan that is very clean, cut at white or denoised; a scan that was rendered or saved again by software that removes noise. The page is then not checked by those three checks, and the dossier says so |
| Signature shape (`signature`) | Two signatures on different documents differ in shape by more than the limit | One person signing differently on different occasions, a pen of another kind, a scan that distorts the strokes, marks over the signature. A signature copied from another document would pass |
| Logo and stamp shape (`mark`) | A logo or a stamp on one document differs in shape, by more than the limit, from the one on another document of the same issuer | One impression differing from another (a stamp pressed askew or in part, ink pressure), a scan that blurs a fine mark, printed matter over it, a re-issued form with a changed mark. A mark copied from another document would pass |
| Reused signature (`reuse`) | A signature on one document is the same image as the one on another document, to within the limit | Software that puts one stored image of a signature on every document it makes, one page submitted in two forms, one scan used for two documents, a plain mark that is not solid (a hollow box, a diagonal stroke) printed the same on two documents; a solid bar, a dot or a block is skipped, and a dashed rule or a rule that is not quite level can fall on either side of that line. A signature that was redrawn or traced is not reported |
| Balance chain (`statement_balance`) | A running balance that does not follow from the rows, beyond the tolerance | A misread digit, a row that was not read, a layout the checks do not assume (newest row first, signed amounts) |
| Statement dates (`statement_dates`) | A date out of order or outside the stated period | A different date convention. Dates are read day first (DD/MM/YYYY) |
| Payslip totals (`payslip_sums`) | Totals that disagree with each other or with the lines under them | A misread digit, a line that was not read, a layout the checks do not assume |
| Net pay in words (`payslip_words`) | The words and the figure for the net pay differ | A misread digit or word |
| Pay date (`payslip_dates`) | A pay date outside the pay month and the month after it | A late or early payroll run, a different date convention, a misread digit |
| Weekday on the application (`application_dates`) | The weekday printed with the date is not the weekday of that date | A slip when the form was filled in, a misread digit |
| Name, address, employer across documents (`cross_name`, `cross_address`, `cross_employer`) | The same value written differently, a Minor mismatch (close but not identical) or a Mismatch | Another word order, a title, an initial, a nickname or abbreviation, a trading name against the registered name, a move, an older address on a re-issued document. On a scan, one letter or digit misread: a Minor mismatch is often only that |
| Account number (`cross_account`) | Account numbers that do not match. A masked number matches by its visible end, at least three characters | A different account, a misread digit |
| Salary payer (`cross_payer`) | No salary credit on the statement names a payer that shares a word with the employer | A trading name, an abbreviation, a group company, a payer the statement does not name. It is a heuristic and can be wrong either way |
| Net pay against the bank (`cross_net_pay`) | The net pay is not among the credits for its month, within the tolerance | A part payment or several credits, a payslip from another account, a misread digit |
| Declared income (`cross_income`) | The declared monthly income differs from the gross earnings by more than the tolerance | Income from another source that the application includes, a different month, a misread digit |
| Declared existing EMI against the statement (`cross_emi`) | The statement shows debits whose narration names an instalment (EMI, instalment or loan) that total more, in one calendar month, than the existing EMI declared on the application, beyond the tolerance | A transfer whose narration only says loan, a payment made for someone else, a loan taken after the application, a declared figure that covers only some of the debts, a misread digit or word. Instalments paid under a lender's name alone are not seen, and instalments split over two statements are not added |
| Dates against the application (`cross_dates`) | A document dated after the application it was submitted with | An application filled in earlier than it was submitted, a re-issued document, a different date convention |
| Duplicate file (`cross_duplicate`) | The same content submitted more than once | An accidental second upload |
| Same producing software (`cross_producer`) | Documents of different kinds made by the same program within a few minutes | One office system issuing both, one print-to-PDF tool used for everything. It appeared on all 24 unedited cases of the [probe](probe.md), whose files were all printed by one program in one sitting |
| Obligations against income (`context_emi`) | Monthly obligations a larger share of declared income than a reference limit | The limit is synthetic and illustrative, not a lending standard. Many ordinary applications are above it |
| Age of a document (`context_age`) | A document dated further before the application than a reference limit | A statement kept from an earlier month |
| Cluster (`link`) | Findings of independent kinds that share a field, a place on a page or a name, address, employer or account | Unrelated findings that happen to share a place are grouped. The count of kinds is a priority for review, not a probability |

Two things apply to every row:

- **A check that could not run is a limitation, not a clear result.** When a page is too poor to read, a field is unreadable or a step fails or times out, the dossier lists what was not checked. The text beside it says to treat that as not checked.
- **Misread digits are the main source of false conflicts on scans.** In the benchmark on synthetic pages the recogniser's own confidence did not separate misread words from correct ones. The tool therefore classes each scanned page by its sharpness, shows a conflict that rests on a doubtful reading as a possible extraction error, and does not trust numbers read from a poor page.

## Limitations and assumptions

These are the lists the tool prints in the dossier appendix and in the Capabilities tab of the review desk. They are written from `src/docforensics/capabilities.json`; to change them, edit the JSON and run `python -m docforensics.capabilities --update` from the repository folder.

### Limitations

1. Every document used to build and test this tool is synthetic. Nothing was evaluated on real documents, real scanners or real layouts.
2. Findings are for a human reviewer. An ordinary cause (a re-issue, a rounding rule, a misread digit, an unusual layout) can produce any of them, and the tool cannot tell which.
3. OCR output is a reading, not ground truth. On scans a number from a degraded or poor page is not trusted, and a conflict that rests on one is shown as a possible extraction error.
4. The statement checks assume the oldest row comes first and that a balance follows each row. A statement listed newest first, or with signed amounts, is reported as a layout the checks do not assume and is not checked.
5. A totals row at the foot of a statement is not read, so it is not checked.
6. A transaction row that extraction misses (for example one with a garbled date on a scan) shows up as a balance break at the next row.
7. The saved-revision comparison reads incremental saves only. By default a linearized PDF is not compared, at most 16 revisions of a file are opened and a page of more than 5000 words is not compared; each of these is reported as a limitation.
8. A PDF that was saved more than once for an ordinary reason (a correction, a re-issue, a form being filled in) shows revision observations too.
9. The cover check looks at opaque rectangular fills, and at pictures smaller than half a page, drawn after the text they lie over. A cover of another form (a curve, a clipped path), text hidden by its colour, and the draw order of a picture on a file whose page content is unusual are not handled; a page that draws more than 5000 objects is not examined and the dossier says so. Invisible text on a page that is mostly one picture is taken as the text layer of a scan and is not reported.
10. The font check compares figures and dates that stand in a text run of their own with the others in their column (four or more) or on their line (three or more). A figure inside a longer run of text is not compared, and bold against regular of one family is not. A logo, stamp or signature placed as a picture on a text page is reported like any other picture. A figure that stands in no column of four and no line of three such values is not compared at all. In a development probe of 24 synthetic cases printed by another program (docs/probe.md), of 77 numbers re-set in the built-in Helvetica 42 stood in such a group and all 42 were flagged; the 35 that did not (the net pay of 24 payslips, the declared income of 6 applications, a statement credit in 3 cases and a debit in 2) were not flagged, and another check found 34 of them. The check flagged none of the 96 numbers re-set in the page's own font.
11. The metadata check reads what the file says about itself: producer and creator names, creation and modification dates. Anyone who can write the file can write them, and a file saved again for an ordinary reason shows a later modification date. It names an editor only from its list of program names. In a development probe of 24 payslips (docs/probe.md), a file that named Acrobat as producer and creator and a file with a modification date 25 minutes after its creation were each reported in 24 of 24, with no place in the document to point to; a producer name that is not on the list (PyMuPDF) and an incremental update that adds a catalog entry and changes nothing a reader sees were reported in 0 of 24 each.
12. The grain check compares each scanned word with the median of its page, on the paper inside the word's box and away from the ink, and leaves out paper brighter than 250 (the scanner cuts it at white). Scanners and JPEG saves change grain for ordinary reasons. On a mildly degraded scan a changed word can keep a grain close to the page's, because the second JPEG save puts back grain of about the same size, so the check finds a patch more easily on coarser scans. Pages of poor quality are not analysed and the dossier says so.
13. The placement check straightens a scanned page by the turn that makes its text rows sharpest, then compares each word's position with its row, and a figure's right edge and height with the page's other figures and its column. The height of a text word is not compared (its box depends on its letters and on the slant of its line). A difference counts when it is more than 6 px (position in the row) or 10 px (figure height, right edge): four times a spread that is never taken below 1.5, 2.5 and 2.5 px. Those floors come from the largest differences on 42 genuine simulated statements (3.3, 7.0 and 6.1 px on pages not degraded), and the largest right-edge difference rose from 5.6 to 6.1 px between the first six and the next eight, so a genuine statement can still give an observation, and a shift under the limits, or a row pasted from a scan turned the same way, is not seen. On a degraded page the floors are raised by half and figure columns are rarely found (one to five figures read), so the right-edge comparison is in practice not made there. A word on a line of fewer than four words (a label and its value in a header) has no row to be measured against, so a paste there is seen only when a grain, arithmetic or cross-document check disagrees. A creased or curled page moves words and can give an observation. A page of poor quality, one with fewer than 12 words or no row of four words, or one turned by more than 2.9 degrees is not compared and the dossier says so. The turn is looked for up to 10 degrees either way; a page turned further than that is not recognised as turned and may be compared as if it were turned less, except a page that stands on its side or upside down (limitation 36).
14. A revision boundary is recognised from a startxref pointer followed by %%EOF. A file crafted to carry a convincing pointer inside a content stream can mislead it, which gives a false observation or a visible limitation, not a silent pass.
15. A document the tool does not recognise as one of the three supported types is left out of the comparisons across documents, and the dossier says which comparisons were not made. Wording unlike the specs' can read as unrecognised.
16. Names, addresses and employers are graded, not only passed or flagged: the same value apart from case, spacing and punctuation passes; the same words in another order, with other titles or with initials (names), the same comma-separated parts of an address in another order, an employer with another legal form, and the same letters with a space more or less inside a word (a scan can split 'IRONWOOD' into 'IRONW OOD'), are an observation 'written differently'; text at or above the similarity limit (0.85 for names, 0.8 for addresses and employers) that is still not identical is a 'Minor mismatch'; anything further apart is a 'Mismatch'. A name with a whole word left out (such as a middle name) is a Minor mismatch whatever its similarity, as long as two words or more are left; a lone first name against a full name is a Mismatch. The limits are starting values and were not tuned. In the held-out run 6 of the 7 planted changes of a name, address or employer were found; the miss is a major address change on a 120 dpi scan (eval/test.md).
17. An initial stands for any name it starts, so 'M. Varghese' and 'Mohan Varghese' are reported only as written differently. An address with the same words in another order but without commas is a Minor mismatch or a Mismatch by how far apart the text is, never a difference in form; any change in the numbers of an address, or in their order, is a Mismatch. An employer with a word more or fewer apart from Pvt, Ltd and the like ('(P)' counts as Pvt, a bare P as a word) is a Minor mismatch at a text similarity of 0.8 or more and a Mismatch below it.
18. Amounts are not graded: a net pay that differs from the credit by no more than the larger of INR 1 and 0.5% of the larger amount, a declared income within 5% of the gross earnings, and a sum within a document that is off by INR 1 or less pass without a finding.
19. On a scan a Minor mismatch of a name or an address can be one letter misread by the OCR. On a page of good quality it is shown as a conflict with a note saying so; on a degraded or poor page it is shown as a possible extraction error to verify. Account numbers have no minor grade: they match, or they are a Mismatch.
20. That the employer pays the salary is judged by the first word of the employer's name (and the first two words joined, in case a scan split the first word) against the words of the descriptions of salary credits on the statement (and neighbouring words joined). It is a heuristic and can be wrong in either direction.
21. An account number that is masked, or short, matches another by its visible end, which must be at least three characters.
22. A payslip dated outside every statement period submitted is reported as not compared, not as a conflict. A set of several months' statements looks the same.
23. The same software producing different kinds of document within ten minutes is noted, and it also happens for ordinary reasons (one office system issuing both).
24. The context rules use reference limits that are synthetic and illustrative (obligations up to half of declared income, documents up to 90 days old); they are not a lending standard, and the instalment sum ignores interest.
25. Thresholds were set and checked on a development set of synthetic documents (80 cases from one generator). The test split was run once, afterwards, on the frozen tree, and nothing was tuned to its results: 79 of 96 changes were found and 1 of 144 genuine documents was flagged (eval/test.md, which also names the one fix made after a first look at layout B). Those are the figures of the tool at spec-freeze plus two fixes; the five features added after that are not covered (README, Evaluation). On other scanners, papers or layouts they may not hold; every finding prints the tolerance it used.
26. Untrusted files are opened in the same process as the tool. Size, page and time limits apply, but there is no sandbox.
27. Handwriting is not analysed, and text other than printed English is not read.
28. The signature comparison was fitted on synthetic signatures only, drawn by the generator for 14 invented people, and its limit was set from them. It looks in a fixed area above the caption printed under the signature, sized by the caption's text, so a layout that signs elsewhere is reported as not compared or compares the wrong marks; a stamp or a ruled line in that area counts as part of the signature unless it fills the area or reaches its edge, which is reported as not compared. A speck of ink under 12 px² is dropped, and so is a small separate piece (under a tenth of the largest) that sticks out of the box of the larger pieces; a stray larger than that, or one inside the box of the signature, counts as part of it, and in a scan of 120 dpi the drop can take off the end of a thin real stroke. A scan turned by more than 2.9 degrees is not compared. It reads shape, not authorship: one person's signatures vary from signing to signing, a scan or another kind of pen changes the strokes, and a signature copied onto another document passes. A mismatch is a lead for a reviewer and nothing more.
29. Findings are linked only through a shared field, an overlapping place on a page, or the same name, address, employer or account value. Two unrelated findings that share a place are grouped, and a large shape drawn over several fields can join the findings on all of them, so a group can be wider than the story behind it. Findings that a person would relate by meaning but that share none of these are not linked. A group of two kinds of check can have an ordinary cause, and its review priority is a count of kinds of check, not a likelihood. The overlap limit (half of the smaller box) is a starting value that was not tuned, and the evaluation scores findings, not groups, so it has no measured accuracy.
30. In the explanation calls a language model, if one is given, only words the headline of a group of findings and reads the case once for relations between findings, a short summary and a reading order; there it does not detect, read the pages or change, add or remove a finding (its separate job of reading pages of an unsupported type is the limitation on template-free reading, further down this list). The prompt carries finding titles and field values, which come from the documents, so a document can put its own text in front of the model; the guard, the fixed JSON shape and the label are what limit it. The guard checks ids, figures, dates and verdict words, not meaning, and a number written in words is not checked. Accepted wording was seen wrong: a headline that called the revision text-change finding F-001 'the debit amount of 723.26' (end-to-end run), an amount in words given as another figure, '$' written for rupees, a revision change called 'the transactions listed'. A relation's free-text reason can name the wrong document or field though its kind is checked. Measured on development-set prompts only, CPU only, one run each, a small sample: of 24 headline wordings the guard accepted 18 from phi4-mini and 13 from qwen3-4b-instruct; reading a case, 9 of 26 relations were kept from phi4-mini (5 readings) and 19 of 23 from qwen3 (10 readings). A call took 17 to 26 s on average for a headline and 40 to 48 s for a reading (the longest 112 s), and the model is loaded for every call, so a case with a model takes a minute or more, and free RAM fell to about 2 GiB. A reply is cut at 1024 tokens, and phi4-mini was seen to write on without end in 2 of 16 readings before the reply format bounded its lists. Not measured: GPU use, a vision-language model on page images, and any model on the held-out split.
31. The logo and stamp comparison was fitted on synthetic marks only: six invented designs of each, drawn by the generator, with both limits set from them. Two of the six logos, which share a motif and differ only in the outline, are not told apart at any limit; a small change to a logo (one point moved a tenth of its width) is not found; and the margin at the logo limit is narrow (the largest genuine pair is 6.5 against a limit of 10.5, the nearest pair of designs that can be told apart 13.8). It looks in a fixed window right of the line that names the issuer (the topmost line of page 1 that reads as a name among those within 15 % of the tallest) and above the caption printed under the stamp, sized by the text beside it, so a layout that puts a mark elsewhere is reported as not compared or compares the wrong ink; a window that is mostly ink or has ink at its edge is reported as not compared. The same two drops of ink apply as for signatures. Under a short issuer name the line beneath it runs on into the logo window: its words are erased from the window, and where that line stops inside the logo (or text is printed over the logo itself) the logo is reported as not compared, because the two documents would cut it differently. Documents count as one issuer when that line reads alike (80 % likeness), so a misread or changed name leaves a document ungrouped and unchecked, and a title more than 15 % taller than the issuer's name, printed alike on the documents of two issuers, takes the place of the name and groups them, so their different marks are reported as a difference. When two documents carry a mark but no two share an issuer line, that is one limitation; a single marked document says nothing. A scan turned by more than 2.9 degrees is not compared, and the round stamp of a payslip is not compared. It reads shape, not origin: a mark copied from another document passes.
32. A scanned page is classed good, degraded or poor by its sharpness alone, with two limits set on the simulated scans of one generator. A page from another scanner or with other content can fall on the other side of a limit: a blurred page called good has its digits trusted, a sharp one called degraded has its conflicts shown as limitations to verify. Real scanners are not simulated.
33. The OCR can misread a letter or a digit with no sign of doubt. The tool does not repair what it reads, and a value read from a page classed good carries the confidence of that class (up to 0.95), so a wrong one is not marked as doubtful. The evaluation counts these in its 'wrong, trusted' column: in the held-out run 16 text values (4 and 4 on layout A, mild and 150 dpi; 3 and 5 on layout B) and no amount, date or id.
34. Extraction depends on the wordings of labels, column headings and captions that the spec of each document type lists. A wording that is not listed leaves its value missing, and whatever depends on that value is left unchecked; the tool says so as a limitation and does not guess. On the held-out layout B, about half of whose wordings the specs do not list, digital pages gave 91 % of amounts, 97 % of dates, 34 % of text values and no id, and the checks within a document could not be made in full on any of its 24 genuine statements and 24 genuine payslips (eval/test.md). A document that words its labels differently from the specs will meet the same limit, and a value under a label that is not listed cannot be compared with another document's. A development probe of 24 synthetic cases printed by another program (docs/probe.md) showed the same: the pay date, labelled Paid On in 12 of the 24 payslips, and the net pay in words, printed on all 24, were not read, so for those 12 payslips the net pay was not compared with the statement's credit, and the dossier of every payslip said that it could not fully verify it.
35. The grain and placement checks found few changed scans. In the held-out split they found 3 of the 16 scans that were changed by pasting from another scan or by erasing and retyping (the placement check 1 of the 8 pasted scans, the grain check 2 of the 8 retyped ones), arithmetic and cross-document checks found 3 more, and 10 were not found at all; at 120 dpi 2 of 9 changes of any kind were found. A scan on which the tool raised nothing therefore says little about whether it was changed. These checks also gave the one flag on a genuine document, a 120 dpi scan (eval/test.md).
36. The reader does not turn a page back upright. It recognises two cases, and reads such a page as poor quality with the dossier saying why. A page on its side (a scan turned a quarter turn, or a PDF page stored turned 90 or 270 degrees) is found because most of its words are taller than wide. A page upside down is found because the angle test of the OCR engine turns most of the lines it looks at (a scan), or because most of the words of the text layer run right to left (a PDF page stored or drawn turned half a turn); at least 12 lines or words are needed to decide. Its values carry low confidence, and the checks that compare them, the placement of its words and its marks are not run on it. The rules were added after cases with one such page gave false conflicts: the page's rows are the document's columns (on its side) or come in the wrong order (upside down), so a value pairs with the wrong label. In 4 genuine development cases of three documents (2 scans, 2 PDFs), a statement turned a quarter gave a false address conflict with the other documents and a payslip turned a quarter gave false employer and payer conflicts, in every case; with the rule, the turned page gave no flag in any of them. Before the half-turn rule, a statement scanned upside down gave a false placement observation in 1 of the 2 scans, and a statement stored as a PDF turned half a turn gave a false account-number conflict with the other documents in both PDFs; with it, a genuine statement, payslip and application each stored turned half a turn, and a statement scanned turned half a turn at the mild level, gave no flag. Measured on development data (probe documents and simulated scans, never a held-out split): the half-turn rule fired on none of 182 upright pages (57 scanned pages, 77 PDF pages, 48 simulated scans; the angle test turned at most 5 % of the lines of an upright page). It found 76 of 77 PDF pages stored turned half a turn (the miss has 10 words of three characters or more, under the minimum) and 32 of 48 simulated scans turned half a turn: 16 of 16 at the mild level, 16 of 16 at 150 dpi and none of 16 at 120 dpi, where the angle test turns 4 to 35 % of the lines and is unsure of the rest. The engine's own bar for a line (0.9) is used; a lower one finds more of the 120 dpi pages (15 of 16 at 0.5) and was not adopted, because its limit would be set on the 16 pages it is then judged on. A quarter turn was found in 96 of 96 simulated scans. A scan turned half a turn that is not found is read as it stands: in 4 genuine statements scanned at 120 dpi and turned, 8 of 68 fields were found, the dossier said that no table was found, there was no false conflict, and 1 of the 4 gave a false visual observation (the learned word check). Not found: any other angle, a page with fewer than 12 words or lines, and a page of which only part is turned; a page in a script written right to left would be taken for upside down (the tool reads printed English only). No page in the development or the held-out split is turned, so none of this is in the reported figures. A page should be turned upright before it is submitted.
37. The entity view puts two writings together by the pairwise grading of the cross-document checks, which is not transitive. A writing joins the first entity, in the order the documents mention them, in which every writing is graded as the same as it or as the same apart from form, and is an entity of its own if there is none, so two writings graded as a mismatch are never in one entity. A writing that fits more than one entity sits in the first, so which entities there are, and where such a writing is put, depend on the order the documents are read in. An initial stands for any name it starts, so 'M. Varghese' fits both 'Mohan Varghese' and 'Manoj Varghese' (a Minor mismatch with each other) and sits with the one read first, and a masked account number fits any number that ends the same way. Only names, addresses, employers and account numbers are entities. Dates, amounts and ids are not, and neither are aliases, nicknames, a person named by role ('the borrower') or a name inside running text. A value the extraction missed is absent from the view, and one it misread is an entity of its own, so a letter misread in a name appears as two names. A finding touches an entity only through a field it cites or a box it lies over, so one that concerns an entity in some other way is not drawn on it, and every finding that does touch one is drawn whatever its review status. The view has no accuracy measured, because the evaluation scores findings and not entities, and it does not say that two entities are, or are not, one person or one thing.
38. The signed checkpoints show that the audit trail still reads as it did when a checkpoint was signed. They do not show who held the key or when: the private key is a plain file without a password in the signing folder of the data folder, the time in a checkpoint is the clock of the machine that signed it, and no outside party stamps it. Whoever can read that folder can change the trail, work the hashes out again and sign it with a key of their own; a reader tells that apart only by knowing the public key from somewhere else (verify-audit --pubkey).
39. A case folder whose checkpoints.jsonl was removed reads as unsigned, which verify-audit reports and passes unless a public key is given. A reviewer action does not sign such a case afresh, but the next run of it signs the trail as it stands, with the key of the data folder. The last checkpoints can also be deleted together with the events they cover; what is left still holds, and the next action signs it. Likewise the events after the last checkpoint, which are counted as not covered and not as changed, can be replaced before the next action signs them. A copy of a checkpoint or of the head that verify-audit printed, kept outside the case folder, shows the cut; only a copy of the head, taken while those events were there, shows the replacement. A checkpoint carries the public key that signed it, so without a key known beforehand it shows only that one key signed this trail.
40. The tool makes no claim of compliance with SOC 2, ISO/IEC 27001, ISO/IEC 27037 or any other standard. The audit trail and its signed checkpoints are checks of integrity that a reader can repeat; a standard also asks about people, procedure and the custody of the original files, which software cannot supply.
41. Template-free reading by the model (--llm) is inference. The model decides which words are a fact and what to call it; code checks only that the words are on the page and that the value is inside them. The name is not checked: a bank account number was named invoice_number in the measurement. The value is the page's own text, so a letter or digit the OCR misread is copied as misread (a decimal comma read as a point gave 1.150.00) and nothing repairs it. Such a field has a confidence below the doubt limit whatever the page quality, feeds no check and no comparison across documents, and raises no finding: a page of an unsupported type is listed, not examined for inconsistencies, and the type is not the model's call.
42. Template-free reading was measured on one pack made for it: 7 synthetic cases of an invoice and an electricity bill in two layouts, one run per model, on the development split; the held-out ext-test split was not read by any model. Two defects of the code that reads the model were found and fixed on that data, and the figures before and after are both in docs/spike-x-extraction.md. phi4-mini (97 of 182 facts right) joined two labels and their two values into single fields on layout B, missed the tax amount of 2 of 7 invoices and the closing meter reading of all three layout B bills, and left 9 of 15 facts of a 120 dpi bill unread; qwen3-4b-instruct-2507 (38 of 156 facts right on the 6 cases it finished) put the label inside the value and gave one reply that was not JSON. Other documents, other languages, longer pages and other models are untested.
43. The model is shown at most the first 60 lines of each of the first 2 pages of a document, and the first 60 items of its reply are used (the prompt asks for 20, and phi4-mini gave 21 to 25 on 4 of 14 pages); what is left out is counted and said as a limitation in the dossier. A call takes 60 to 123 s a page on this CPU (mean 88 s), the model is loaded for each call, and after one failed call the rest of the case is not read by the model. It is given the text of the page only, never boxes or images.
44. Compression-history analysis of scanned pages was set up on the first 36 cases of one synthetic pack (payslips, one generator, one JPEG writer), so its figures are in-sample; 72 further cases of the same generator gave the same result, which shows that the figures do not depend on those 36 draws, not that they hold on other scans. Its limit for an earlier save (a dip of 1.3) lies in a gap that is empty in this pack: pages without an earlier save measure 1.015 to 1.032 (24 pages: 12 saved once, 1.015 to 1.030, and 12 saved twice at the same quality; 6 more saved once at quality 35 cannot be measured) and the 78 pages with one 1.82 or more, so a real page with a weak trace between them is not covered by any measurement. The held-out ext-test split was not run on it.
45. Compression-history analysis sees nothing when the page was saved once, when the last save is less than 10 quality points above the earlier one (which includes a last save at a lower quality than the earlier one, as when a mailer shrinks a good scan: invisible by design), or when the changed part has the page's own JPEG grid and quality; the project's own splice of a part of another scan of the same level is of that kind, and on the dev split a region appeared on 1 of its 5 scans (cause not examined, not claimed as a find) while on another the history was found and no region. A page whose last save is under quality 40 is not examined: the lowest quality tried is 30, so there is no earlier save to look for; the absence of a region on such a page is not a result (21 of the 90 unchanged dev scans, 2 of 12 and 4 of 24 in the pack). It found none of 18 edits whose earlier save was written with Photoshop-family quantisation tables instead of libjpeg's, so it may depend on the earlier save having been written by libjpeg; whether real scanners and programs do so is not known. The earlier quality it names is where the trace shows, in steps of 5: with earlier saves at 82, 47 and 33 it was exactly right on 0 of 16.
46. A compression-history observation is not a statement that anything was changed: a page put together from more than one scan, and a stamp, date, page number or sticker that the scanner or a program added later, leave the same mark. A picture that is not the saved JPEG (a PNG, a scaled or turned picture) and the rendered page of a scanned PDF (the embedded picture is not taken out) are listed as not examined, and a page read from the text layer is not examined by it.
47. The reused-signature check was set on the signature pairs of one synthetic pack (one generator): its limit of 5 % lies above 23 of the 24 copies and below all 47 pairs from separate signings, so the figures are in-sample. The 24th copy lies 6.7 % away (a scan at 120 dpi) and is not found; the nearest separate pair lies 8.8 % away, 3.8 points above the limit. A limit of 8 % would have found all 24 and flagged none of the 47, with 0.8 points to the nearest separate pair instead of 3.8; the limit was not moved to fit the one miss on one pack. A person who signs very much alike every time, a signature stamp, software that puts a stored image of a signature on each document, or a plain mark that is not solid (a hollow box, a diagonal stroke) printed the same on two documents will be reported; a solid bar, a dot or a block is skipped, and a dashed rule or a rule that is not quite level can fall on either side of that line. A copy that was scanned at a low resolution can fall above the limit (one of 24 did, at 120 dpi); a signature that was redrawn or traced is not reported. Stamps and logos are not compared for sameness (at the limit of its first attempt, 18 of 48 separate synthetic stamp pressings were flagged, and 27 lie within the 5 % used here, so a rule that flagged reused stamps would have flagged separate ones); whether real stamps would separate is not known. Only documents of a type with a signature caption in its spec are compared. The held-out ext-test split was not run on it.
48. Reviewer decisions are counted, not learned from: a dismissal is one person's judgement and is not proof that a finding was wrong, a finding nobody has acted on counts as open, and the counts cover whatever was reviewed, by whoever reviewed it, so they are not accuracy figures. No threshold or detector changes because of them; a person reads the counts and edits the thresholds file.
49. The raw file-structure check reads names, positions and sizes from the stored bytes. It does not check any signature's cryptography, does not open, decode or run embedded files, scripts or actions, and is not a malware scanner; a signed PDF always carries a limitation that says the signature was not verified.
50. Every fact the raw file-structure check reports also occurs in ordinary files (a program that pads a file or writes a trailer, saving again in place, a second signature, a countersignature or a form filled in after a signature, an attachment or an /OpenAction written by a generator, an /Info record that differs from the XMP packet, an editor used to crop a genuine photograph), and anyone who can write a file can write each of these fields. Nothing reported means only that none of these facts was found.
51. The figures for the raw file-structure check are by construction: 120 files from one generator, built by the author of the check with the check in view (60 untouched, 60 with one planted change). They show what the check does with the changes it was written for, are not a detection rate, and say nothing about files made by other programs.
52. The raw file-structure check does not look at bytes before the PDF header (such a file is turned away at upload), file-attachment annotations, text chunks stored after the image data of a PNG, or a JPEG picture inside a PDF that has another filter in front of DCT. The list of image-editor names is fixed and matched at the start of a word ('canva' also matches 'Canvas'), so a program with a similar name is named too and an editor that is not on the list is not. Pictures past the first 50 of a PDF and objects past the first 20,000 are not followed (a limitation says so). A document the read step could not open gets no byte-level check.
53. The learned word check compares each measurable word of a scan with the other words of its page and lists the words whose weighted score over fourteen measures reaches a limit. The score is not a probability and does not say that anything was changed: it says how much a word looks like the synthetic edits the weights were fitted to. Uneven lighting, a stamp, a fold, a second JPEG save, another typeface or size, and a field filled in by another program move the same measures.
54. The learned word check was fitted to edits made by this project's own generator (parts of other scans pasted in, pasted fields and blocks, the same place of another scan pasted in, retyped amounts) on scans from the project's scan simulator (JPEG, three levels of quality), and measured on held-out pages from the same generator: 16 of 36 and 59 of 72 changed pages found, none of the 195 unedited pages flagged. The generator, the edits and the check have one author, so the figures show that these edits are found, not that edits made by anyone else, on other scanners, at other resolutions or in other file formats, would be. No real scan was used. None of 195 unedited pages flagged does not exclude a rate of about 1.5 % of unedited pages flagged. Of the generator's two page layouts, the changed pages measured are those of layout A and the payslips of the compression-history pack (in both layouts); edits on statements and applications of layout B were not measured, and 72 genuine scan pages of layout B tried afterwards were not flagged, which says nothing about edited ones.
55. The learned word check finds some kinds of edit less often than others: on the held-out pack pages, pasted blocks 18 of 18 and pasted fields 17 of 18, but the same place of another scan pasted in (kind aligned) 12 of 18 and retyped amounts 12 of 18, which the grain and placement checks find 18 of 18. For parts of another scan pasted into a page, most of its gain over those two checks comes from two hand-written measures of how far a word sits off the line of its neighbours (alone they find 13 of 72 development pages; the other twelve measures alone find none). A change that leaves a word's grain, edges, strokes, JPEG grid and place as the page's own is not seen.
56. The learned word check looks only at pages read by OCR that are not of poor quality, and only at words with enough paper in their box to measure; a page with fewer than 12 measurable words is not compared, and a limitation says so for each such document. It does not look at digital PDFs. It lists at most eight words a page.
57. A table row is a line whose first cell has the shape the column asks for (a date, or an amount). A cell with that shape that cannot be read as one, such as the amount 57.500.00, the date O3/05/2026 or a date with a month over 12, stays a row with that cell unreadable: the statement is reported as not fully verifiable and the value is named, an amount that cannot be read leaves the stretch of the balance chain that holds it untested, and a date that cannot be read leaves that row out of the date checks. A row whose first cell has no such shape at all (a letter among the digits of a date written with a month's name, such as 03-Mar-2O26, a date split into words, a missing separator, two different separators, a year of one digit) is left out of the table, and the balance chain can then show a break that is really a row that was not read; the explanation of that finding lists this among the possible causes.
58. A balance printed with Dr (for example 5,000.00 Dr) is read as money owed, that is minus, and one printed with Cr as money held; the marker on a debit or credit amount only names the column and gives no sign. A statement with one Amount column and, beside it, a column that marks each line Dr or Cr is read only when its heading names neither a debit nor a credit column and the wordings are among those the bank statement spec lists (Amount, Txn Amount, Transaction Amount or Amt for the amounts; Dr/Cr, Cr/Dr, Dr / Cr, Cr / Dr, Dr Cr or Debit/Credit for the marks; and, as the mark itself, Dr, DR., (Dr) or Debit, and Cr, CR., (Cr) or Credit): the amount goes to the side its mark names and the other side is found empty. A mark of any other wording, an amount with no mark and a mark with no amount leave that row's debit and credit unread (reported missing), the balance steps across the row are not tested and the checks say they cannot fully verify the statement; so does an amount cell that holds two numbers, as when a new amount is laid over the old one. The reader was written after a probe of six synthetic statements of this layout, so those six say how it behaves where it was aimed and nothing about statements in general; no held-out split holds a statement of this layout, and it has been run on drawn pages only. Amounts with a minus sign and no marker are the signed layout that the statement checks do not assume, stated above.
59. The declared-EMI check reads a debit as an instalment only when its narration holds the whole word EMI, instalment (also spelt installment, or instl) or loan, and none of the words fee, charge, penalty, GST, tax, bounce, return, refund, reversal, waiver, insurance, premium, closure, foreclosure or prepayment. An instalment paid under a lender's name alone, or written in a way these words do not cover, is not seen (none of the 4 such cases of the pack was found), and a transfer whose narration says loan, or a payment made for someone else, can be counted as an instalment. On one statement the check takes the calendar month with the most instalments, and it takes one statement at a time, so instalments split over two statements are not added together. A declared figure above the instalments is never reported, since an instalment paid from another account or in cash has an ordinary cause.
60. The declared-EMI check cannot read a narration when the statement reader does not list the heading of that column. The generator's layout B heads it 'Narration', which the reader leaves out on purpose (layout B is the test of wording the reader has not seen), so there every narration is empty: 6 of the 6 planted gaps in layout B were missed, and for all 22 layout-B cases of the pack the dossier said that the narration could not be read and that any instalment among the debits was not compared. A statement that heads the column with a wording the reader does not list would be treated the same way: a limitation, and no comparison.
61. The figures for the declared-EMI check are by construction: 44 cases from one generator, built by the author of the check with the check in view, the instalments narrated in the wordings the check reads (EMI, instalment, loan). They show what the check does with those wordings and say nothing about how often a real instalment is narrated like that, or about other layouts, other producers or another language. The statement that an unread narration was not compared was added after the first run, which had missed six cases without a word; both reports are kept, so the second run does not measure the check's reading independently of that fix. The held-out ext-test split was not run on it. A development probe of 24 synthetic cases printed by another program (docs/probe.md) held no statement with an instalment, EMI or loan narration and so did not exercise the check.
62. Pictures that carry no scanner grain (a page printed or rendered to an image) are not given to the grain, placement and learned-word checks, and the dossier says so for each such document. An edit on such a page is found only by the arithmetic and cross-document checks: 88 of 108 edits in a probe of 6 cases printed to 150 dpi png, 200 dpi png and 200 dpi jpg, and none of the three scan checks ever put a finding on an edit there. The line comes from simulated scans, not from a real scanner. The gate was added after that probe had shown the three checks putting an observation on 15 of 18 clean printed pages (0 of 18 after), and its line was set on the same development pages, so those figures show what the gate does with them and not how it behaves on other scanners or other pictures; no held-out split was used. A real scan that is very clean, cut at white or denoised may be taken for such a page and then goes unchecked by those three checks, with the dossier saying so.
63. A number whose glyphs overlap those beside it (a longer amount laid over the currency sign next to it, or over the text under it) is read as no value: the cell is reported missing, the dossier says the statement cannot be fully verified, and no check uses the cell. In a development probe (docs/probe.md), 4 of the 48 edits that re-set a statement credit did this (on 2 of 24 statements), and 3 of the 4 were found by no check.
64. A page changed at its source and printed again by the program that printed the original is a new file with no covered text and no second revision, so the checks of the file have nothing to find; it is found only when a number disagrees with another number in the case. In a development probe of 24 cases (docs/probe.md), a changed declared income, a changed net pay and a changed credit, each alone, were found in 24 of 24; a net pay raised by cutting the tax deduction so that the payslip still adds up, and a credit raised with every later balance carried through, in 12 of 24 each (the 12 whose pay date the tool reads); a debit changed with the balances carried through (8 cases) and a loan amount (8 cases) in none.
65. A table in a page design the readers were not built for is read in part or not at all. In a development probe of 24 unedited cases printed by other programs (docs/probe.md, second probe), 4 of the 7 statement designs and all 5 payslip designs came out that way: one statement design gave 28 of its 328 table cells, and three payslip designs gave none of theirs. The dossier names the values it could not read and says that the statement or payslip cannot be fully verified, and no inconsistency came from those gaps in that set; but a statement whose debits and credits are not read has no balance chain tested, and the other checks that depend on the missing cells are not made. The readers were changed several times to fit this pack, so its figures are not a measure of other documents.
66. Some values are read wrongly at a high confidence and compared by no check. In the second probe (docs/probe.md; 24 unedited cases, digital and as pictures at 150 and 200 dpi): the bank name of a statement read as another line of page 1 (digital: 7 of 24, the rest of the title line, confidence 0.90; each picture pass: 7 of 24, of which 3 the rest of the title line, 3 the period line and 1 the holder's name, confidence 0.69 to 0.90); a payslip deduction name with an amount in front of it (8 digital, 5 at 150 dpi, 7 at 200 dpi); the narration of a transaction that runs over two lines, read in part or merged with the next row (36 digital, 43 at 150 dpi, 55 at 200 dpi, at a confidence up to 1.00); an account number or id with a colon in front, kept as printed (11 in each picture pass, confidence 0.95); a masked account number with its star lost (3 at 200 dpi, confidence 0.67 to 0.81); and a statement period printed as one range whose hyphen a scan glues to the first date, read as the second date alone (2 cases, confidence 0.90), which leaves the end unread so the check of the dates is not made. No check compares a narration, a bank name or a deduction name, and none of these gave a finding.
67. The employer of a payslip and the bank of a statement are read from the tallest line of page 1; when that line holds a label or a column heading of the document type, the title is the tallest line above the first line that holds one. When the first line holds both the bank name and a label (one statement at 150 dpi in the second probe, docs/probe.md), nothing stands above it, and a tall row of the form can be taken as the bank. The bank is compared with nothing, so no finding follows; for a payslip the same shape could give a false employer conflict, which was not seen.
68. The font check reports a design colour as an observation. In the second probe (docs/probe.md) it raised 8 observations in the digital pass, 2 on each of the 4 genuine payslips of a design that prints its two total rows in white on a dark bar ('Value set in a different colour from the values beside it'). They are observations, not inconsistencies, and a reviewer can dismiss them, but the check compares the values in a column and cannot tell a design colour from a changed value.

### Assumptions

1. Dates are read day first (DD/MM/YYYY); a date that could be read another way is marked ambiguous and given a low confidence.
2. Amounts are in rupees, with lakh or western digit grouping.
3. A usable PDF text layer is taken as read; a page whose text layer is unusable is read by OCR instead.
4. Boxes are pixels of the page rendered at 200 dpi unless a page says otherwise; points are pixels x 72 / dpi.
5. Documents are synthetic or authorised for the purpose, and one case is analysed at a time on one machine.
