Appendix C. Limitations and assumptions

Limitations

  • Every document used to build and test this tool is synthetic. Nothing was evaluated on real documents, real scanners or real layouts.
  • Findings are for a human reviewer. An ordinary cause (a re-issue, a rounding rule, a misread digit, an unusual layout) can produce any of them, and the tool cannot tell which.
  • OCR output is a reading, not ground truth. On scans a number from a degraded or poor page is not trusted, and a conflict that rests on one is shown as a possible extraction error.
  • The statement checks assume the oldest row comes first and that a balance follows each row. A statement listed newest first, or with signed amounts, is reported as a layout the checks do not assume and is not checked.
  • A totals row at the foot of a statement is not read, so it is not checked.
  • A transaction row that extraction misses (for example one with a garbled date on a scan) shows up as a balance break at the next row.
  • The saved-revision comparison reads incremental saves only. By default a linearized PDF is not compared, at most 16 revisions of a file are opened and a page of more than 5000 words is not compared; each of these is reported as a limitation.
  • A PDF that was saved more than once for an ordinary reason (a correction, a re-issue, a form being filled in) shows revision observations too.
  • The cover check looks at opaque rectangular fills, and at pictures smaller than half a page, drawn after the text they lie over. A cover of another form (a curve, a clipped path), text hidden by its colour, and the draw order of a picture on a file whose page content is unusual are not handled; a page that draws more than 5000 objects is not examined and the dossier says so. Invisible text on a page that is mostly one picture is taken as the text layer of a scan and is not reported.
  • The font check compares figures and dates that stand in a text run of their own with the others in their column (four or more) or on their line (three or more). A figure inside a longer run of text is not compared, and bold against regular of one family is not. A logo, stamp or signature placed as a picture on a text page is reported like any other picture.
  • The metadata check reads what the file says about itself: producer and creator names, creation and modification dates. Anyone who can write the file can write them, and a file saved again for an ordinary reason shows a later modification date.
  • The grain check compares each scanned word with the median of its page, on the paper inside the word's box and away from the ink, and leaves out paper brighter than 250 (the scanner cuts it at white). Scanners and JPEG saves change grain for ordinary reasons. On a mildly degraded scan a changed word can keep a grain close to the page's, because the second JPEG save puts back grain of about the same size, so the check finds a patch more easily on coarser scans. Pages of poor quality are not analysed and the dossier says so.
  • The placement check straightens a scanned page by the turn that makes its text rows sharpest, then compares each word's position with its row, and a figure's right edge and height with the page's other figures and its column. The height of a text word is not compared (its box depends on its letters and on the slant of its line). A difference counts when it is more than 6 px (position in the row) or 10 px (figure height, right edge): four times a spread that is never taken below 1.5, 2.5 and 2.5 px. Those floors come from the largest differences on 42 genuine simulated statements (3.3, 7.0 and 6.1 px on pages not degraded), and the largest right-edge difference rose from 5.6 to 6.1 px between the first six and the next eight, so a genuine statement can still give an observation, and a shift under the limits, or a row pasted from a scan turned the same way, is not seen. On a degraded page the floors are raised by half and figure columns are rarely found (one to five figures read), so the right-edge comparison is in practice not made there. A word on a line of fewer than four words (a label and its value in a header) has no row to be measured against, so a paste there is seen only when a grain, arithmetic or cross-document check disagrees. A creased or curled page moves words and can give an observation. A page of poor quality, one with fewer than 12 words or no row of four words, or one turned by more than 2.9 degrees is not compared and the dossier says so. The turn is looked for up to 10 degrees either way; a page turned further than that is not recognised as turned and may be compared as if it were turned less, except a page that stands on its side (limitation 36).
  • A revision boundary is recognised from a startxref pointer followed by %%EOF. A file crafted to carry a convincing pointer inside a content stream can mislead it, which gives a false observation or a visible limitation, not a silent pass.
  • A document the tool does not recognise as one of the three supported types is left out of the comparisons across documents, and the dossier says which comparisons were not made. Wording unlike the specs' can read as unrecognised.
  • Names, addresses and employers are graded, not only passed or flagged: the same value apart from case, spacing and punctuation passes; the same words in another order, with other titles or with initials (names), the same comma-separated parts of an address in another order, or an employer with another legal form, are an observation 'written differently'; text at or above the similarity limit (0.85 for names, 0.8 for addresses and employers) that is still not identical is a 'Minor mismatch'; anything further apart is a 'Mismatch'. A name with a word left out (a middle name) is a Minor mismatch. The limits are starting values and were not tuned. In the held-out run 6 of the 7 planted changes of a name, address or employer were found; the miss is a major address change on a 120 dpi scan (eval/test.md).
  • An initial stands for any name it starts, so 'M. Varghese' and 'Mohan Varghese' are reported only as written differently. An address with the same words in another order but without commas is a Minor mismatch or a Mismatch by how far apart the text is, never a difference in form; any change in the numbers of an address, or in their order, is a Mismatch. An employer with a word more or fewer apart from Pvt, Ltd and the like is a Minor mismatch at a text similarity of 0.8 or more and a Mismatch below it.
  • Amounts are not graded: a net pay that differs from the credit by no more than the larger of INR 1 and 0.5% of the larger amount, a declared income within 5% of the gross earnings, and a sum within a document that is off by INR 1 or less pass without a finding.
  • On a scan a Minor mismatch of a name or an address can be one letter misread by the OCR. On a page of good quality it is shown as a conflict with a note saying so; on a degraded or poor page it is shown as a possible extraction error to verify. Account numbers have no minor grade: they match, or they are a Mismatch.
  • That the employer pays the salary is judged by one word of the employer's name against the descriptions of salary credits on the statement. It is a heuristic and can be wrong in either direction.
  • An account number that is masked, or short, matches another by its visible end, which must be at least three characters.
  • A payslip dated outside every statement period submitted is reported as not compared, not as a conflict. A set of several months' statements looks the same.
  • The same software producing different kinds of document within ten minutes is noted, and it also happens for ordinary reasons (one office system issuing both).
  • The context rules use reference limits that are synthetic and illustrative (obligations up to half of declared income, documents up to 90 days old); they are not a lending standard, and the instalment sum ignores interest.
  • Thresholds were set and checked on a development set of synthetic documents (80 cases from one generator). The test split was run once, afterwards, on the frozen tree, and nothing was tuned to its results: 79 of 96 changes were found and 1 of 144 genuine documents was flagged (eval/test.md, which also names the one fix made after a first look at layout B). On other scanners, papers or layouts they may not hold; every finding prints the tolerance it used.
  • Untrusted files are opened in the same process as the tool. Size, page and time limits apply, but there is no sandbox.
  • Handwriting is not analysed, and text other than printed English is not read.
  • The signature comparison was fitted on synthetic signatures only, drawn by the generator for 14 invented people, and its limit was set from them. It looks in a fixed area above the caption printed under the signature, sized by the caption's text, so a layout that signs elsewhere is reported as not compared or compares the wrong marks; a stamp or a ruled line in that area counts as part of the signature unless it fills the area or reaches its edge, which is reported as not compared. A speck of ink under 12 px² is dropped, and so is a small separate piece (under a tenth of the largest) that sticks out of the box of the larger pieces; a stray larger than that, or one inside the box of the signature, counts as part of it, and in a scan of 120 dpi the drop can take off the end of a thin real stroke. A scan turned by more than 2.9 degrees is not compared. It reads shape, not authorship: one person's signatures vary from signing to signing, a scan or another kind of pen changes the strokes, and a signature copied onto another document passes. A mismatch is a lead for a reviewer and nothing more.
  • Findings are linked only through a shared field, an overlapping place on a page, or the same name, address, employer or account value. Two unrelated findings that share a place are grouped, and a large shape drawn over several fields can join the findings on all of them, so a group can be wider than the story behind it. Findings that a person would relate by meaning but that share none of these are not linked. A group of two kinds of check can have an ordinary cause, and its review priority is a count of kinds of check, not a likelihood. The overlap limit (half of the smaller box) is a starting value that was not tuned, and the evaluation scores findings, not groups, so it has no measured accuracy.
  • A language model, if one is given, only words the headline of a group of findings and reads the case once for relations between findings, a short summary and a reading order; it does not detect, read the pages or change, add or remove a finding. The prompt carries finding titles and field values, which come from the documents, so a document can put its own text in front of the model; the guard, the fixed JSON shape and the label are what limit it. The guard checks ids, figures, dates and verdict words, not meaning, and a number written in words is not checked. Accepted wording was seen wrong: a headline that called the revision text-change finding F-001 'the debit amount of 723.26' (end-to-end run), an amount in words given as another figure, '$' written for rupees, a revision change called 'the transactions listed'. A relation's free-text reason can name the wrong document or field though its kind is checked. Measured on development-set prompts only, CPU only, one run each, a small sample: of 24 headline wordings the guard accepted 18 from phi4-mini and 13 from qwen3-4b-instruct; reading a case, 9 of 26 relations were kept from phi4-mini (5 readings) and 19 of 23 from qwen3 (10 readings). A call took 17 to 26 s on average for a headline and 40 to 48 s for a reading (the longest 112 s), and the model is loaded for every call, so a case with a model takes a minute or more, and free RAM fell to about 2 GiB. A reply is cut at 1024 tokens, and phi4-mini was seen to write on without end in 2 of 16 readings before the reply format bounded its lists. Not measured: GPU use, a vision-language model on page images, and any model on the held-out split.
  • The logo and stamp comparison was fitted on synthetic marks only: six invented designs of each, drawn by the generator, with both limits set from them. Two of the six logos, which share a motif and differ only in the outline, are not told apart at any limit; a small change to a logo (one point moved a tenth of its width) is not found; and the margin at the logo limit is narrow (the largest genuine pair is 6.5 against a limit of 10.5, the nearest pair of designs that can be told apart 13.8). It looks in a fixed window right of the line that names the issuer (the topmost line of page 1 that reads as a name among those within 15 % of the tallest) and above the caption printed under the stamp, sized by the text beside it, so a layout that puts a mark elsewhere is reported as not compared or compares the wrong ink; a window that is mostly ink or has ink at its edge is reported as not compared. The same two drops of ink apply as for signatures. Under a short issuer name the line beneath it runs on into the logo window: its words are erased from the window, and where that line stops inside the logo (or text is printed over the logo itself) the logo is reported as not compared, because the two documents would cut it differently. Documents count as one issuer when that line reads alike (80 % likeness), so a misread or changed name leaves a document ungrouped and unchecked, and a title more than 15 % taller than the issuer's name, printed alike on the documents of two issuers, takes the place of the name and groups them, so their different marks are reported as a difference. When two documents carry a mark but no two share an issuer line, that is one limitation; a single marked document says nothing. A scan turned by more than 2.9 degrees is not compared, and the round stamp of a payslip is not compared. It reads shape, not origin: a mark copied from another document passes.
  • A scanned page is classed good, degraded or poor by its sharpness alone, with two limits set on the simulated scans of one generator. A page from another scanner or with other content can fall on the other side of a limit: a blurred page called good has its digits trusted, a sharp one called degraded has its conflicts shown as limitations to verify. Real scanners are not simulated.
  • The OCR can misread a letter or a digit with no sign of doubt. The tool does not repair what it reads, and a value read from a page classed good carries the confidence of that class (up to 0.95), so a wrong one is not marked as doubtful. The evaluation counts these in its 'wrong, trusted' column: in the held-out run 16 text values (4 and 4 on layout A, mild and 150 dpi; 3 and 5 on layout B) and no amount, date or id.
  • Extraction depends on the wordings of labels, column headings and captions that the spec of each document type lists. A wording that is not listed leaves its value missing, and whatever depends on that value is left unchecked; the tool says so as a limitation and does not guess. On the held-out layout B, about half of whose wordings the specs do not list, digital pages gave 91 % of amounts, 97 % of dates, 34 % of text values and no id, and the checks within a document could not be made in full on any of its 24 genuine statements and 24 genuine payslips (eval/test.md). A document that words its labels differently from the specs will meet the same limit, and a value under a label that is not listed cannot be compared with another document's.
  • The grain and placement checks found few changed scans. In the held-out split they found 3 of the 16 scans that were changed by pasting from another scan or by erasing and retyping (the placement check 1 of the 8 pasted scans, the grain check 2 of the 8 retyped ones), arithmetic and cross-document checks found 3 more, and 10 were not found at all; at 120 dpi 2 of 9 changes of any kind were found. A scan on which the tool raised nothing therefore says little about whether it was changed. These checks also gave the one flag on a genuine document, a 120 dpi scan (eval/test.md).
  • The reader does not turn a page back upright. It recognises one case, a page that stands on its side (a scan turned a quarter turn, or a PDF page stored turned 90 or 270 degrees), because most of its words are taller than wide. Such a page is read as poor quality and the dossier says why. Its values carry low confidence, and the checks that compare them, the placement of its words and its marks are not run on it. The rule was added after cases with one such page gave false conflicts: the page's rows are the document's columns, so a value pairs with the wrong label. In 4 genuine development cases of three documents (2 scans, 2 PDFs), a statement turned a quarter gave a false address conflict with the other documents and a payslip turned a quarter gave false employer and payer conflicts, in every case; with the rule, the turned page gave no flag in any of them. A page that is upside down is not recognised (the tool has no test for it). It is read, few of its fields are found (8 to 38 of the 78 to 188 of a statement in those cases), and a check can give a false result: a statement scanned upside down gave a false placement observation in 1 of the 2 scans, and a statement stored as a PDF turned half a turn gave a false account-number conflict with the other documents in both PDFs. No page in the development or the held-out split is turned, so none of this is in the reported figures. A page should be turned upright before it is submitted.

Assumptions

  • Dates are read day first (DD/MM/YYYY); a date that could be read another way is marked ambiguous and given a low confidence.
  • Amounts are in rupees, with lakh or western digit grouping.
  • A usable PDF text layer is taken as read; a page whose text layer is unusable is read by OCR instead.
  • Boxes are pixels of the page rendered at 200 dpi unless a page says otherwise; points are pixels x 72 / dpi.
  • Documents are synthetic or authorised for the purpose, and one case is analysed at a time on one machine.
